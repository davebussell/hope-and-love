EMDR for Trauma & PTSD

EMDR is the therapy most often associated with trauma and PTSD. It can help the brain reprocess overwhelming experiences so they settle into the past, instead of returning as flashbacks, tension, or fear in the present.

When something overwhelming happens, the mind can store the memory unprocessed. The event is over, but the images, emotions, and body sensations stay raw, ready to surface whenever something reminds you of it. EMDR, which stands for Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing, helps the brain finish the processing it could not complete at the time.

What EMDR for trauma can help with

  • Post-traumatic stress (PTSD) and complex PTSD (C-PTSD)
  • Single-incident trauma, such as an accident, assault, or medical event
  • Childhood trauma and experiences that still shape adult life
  • Flashbacks, nightmares, and intrusive memories
  • Hypervigilance and feeling permanently on guard
  • Shame, self-blame, and negative beliefs rooted in the past
  • Trauma connected to loss, betrayal, or relationship harm

How EMDR works with traumatic memory

EMDR uses bilateral stimulation, gentle guided eye movements, taps, or tones, while you briefly hold a memory in mind. This appears to help the brain reprocess the experience, much as it does during dream sleep, so the memory can be stored as something that happened rather than something that is still happening.

One reason many people are drawn to EMDR is that it does not require you to describe every detail of what happened, or to relive it at length. The work is carefully paced, it begins with preparation and grounding, and you stay in control throughout.

Complex and childhood trauma

For trauma that is layered or began early in life, EMDR is usually part of a longer, relationship-centred process. We spend time building safety and inner resources first, then move at a pace that protects your stability. Healing is possible, and it does not have to be rushed.

Common Questions

Frequently asked questions

Does EMDR work for PTSD?
EMDR is one of the most researched therapies for PTSD, and it is recommended for trauma by the World Health Organization and major clinical guidelines. Results vary from person to person, so we set goals together and review progress as we go.
Do I have to talk about the trauma in detail?
No. EMDR does not require you to describe everything that happened or to relive it at length. You stay in control, and we begin with safety and grounding before any reprocessing.
Can EMDR help with childhood or complex trauma?
Yes, though trauma that is layered or began early in life often takes longer. We build stability and inner resources first, then reprocess at a pace that keeps you steady.
How many sessions does EMDR for trauma take?
It depends. A single recent event may resolve in a handful of sessions, while complex trauma usually takes longer. The early sessions focus on preparation before any reprocessing begins.
Is EMDR better than talk therapy for trauma?
They work differently. Talk therapy builds insight and understanding, while EMDR works directly with how a memory is stored in the brain and body. Many people find a combination helpful, and EMDR can be woven in alongside other approaches.
Is EMDR for trauma available virtually or near Belleville?
Online EMDR is available across Ontario, and we offer EMDR for Belleville and the Quinte region by secure video, with in-person sessions in Prince Edward County.
