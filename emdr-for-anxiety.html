EMDR for Anxiety & Panic

Anxiety often has deeper roots. When worry, panic, or dread do not fully settle with talk therapy alone, EMDR can help by working with the earlier experiences and beliefs that keep the alarm system switched on.

Not all anxiety is the same. Some anxiety belongs to the present, and some is held in place by earlier experiences the brain never fully processed. When that is the case, the nervous system can keep responding as though an old threat is still here. EMDR helps the brain reprocess those experiences, so the anxiety they feed can ease.

What EMDR for anxiety can help with

  • Generalized anxiety and persistent worry
  • Panic attacks and the fear of having another one
  • Phobias and specific fears
  • Social anxiety and fear of judgement
  • Performance anxiety and health anxiety
  • Anxiety connected to past trauma or difficult experiences

How EMDR eases anxiety

With EMDR, we look for the experiences and beliefs that sit underneath the anxiety, the moments that first taught your nervous system to brace. Using bilateral stimulation, gentle eye movements, taps, or tones, the brain can reprocess those memories. The beliefs attached to them, such as "I am not safe" or "something bad is about to happen", can then begin to shift.

EMDR does not require you to relive anything in detail, and it is carefully paced. It pairs well with other approaches, including CBT, so therapy can be built around what is actually driving your anxiety.

Common Questions

Frequently asked questions

Can EMDR help anxiety, not just trauma?
Yes. EMDR is best known for trauma, but it can also help with anxiety, panic, and phobias, especially when they are rooted in earlier experiences that have not fully resolved.
How does EMDR reduce anxiety?
It helps the brain reprocess the experiences and beliefs that keep the nervous system on alert, so the body no longer reacts as strongly to old triggers.
Can EMDR help with panic attacks and phobias?
Often, yes. EMDR can target the first or worst experiences linked to a panic response or a phobia, which can reduce how intensely they fire.
How is EMDR different from CBT for anxiety?
CBT works with thoughts and behaviours in the present. EMDR works with how past experiences are stored in the brain and body. Many people benefit from both, and we can combine them.
How many sessions will I need?
It varies with how long-standing the anxiety is and what sits underneath it. We set goals early and review progress together as we go.
Is EMDR for anxiety available online in Ontario?
Yes. Online EMDR is available province-wide by secure video.
